The project

Why we built this and who it is for

Road crashes are one of the leading causes of injury-related hospitalisation in Australia. Deaths get most of the attention, but non-fatal injuries are a far larger and ongoing burden on people, families and hospitals.

The Road Injury Explorer turns official counts from 2011 to 2021 into interactive charts for the public, road safety analysts, government officials and researchers.

Questions it answers

One question for each part of the dashboard

  • Overview: how have hospitalisations changed across Australia?
  • Overview: which road users make up the largest share?
  • People: how do age group and sex change the picture?
  • Location: how do states and territories compare?
  • Location: how do First Nations trends differ by remoteness?

Reading the charts

Three things to keep in mind

Counts are not risk. Larger states usually have larger counts simply because more people live there.

  • Datasets overlap, so never add national, state and First Nations tables together.
  • Deaths are excluded from hospitalisations but included in bed days.
  • Need exact values? Choose View data on any chart.

Data sources

Where the numbers come from

All figures come from the BITRE Hospitalised Injuries from Road Crashes series, built from AIHW hospital data for 2011 to 2021.

  • National: trends, road users, age and sex.
  • State and territory: counts and bed days.
  • First Nations: by remoteness.

How we prepared it

From spreadsheets to charts

  1. Kept only road traffic injury records in KNIME.
  2. Checked exact duplicates and standardised fields.
  3. Built one combined file of 638 records, labelled by data type.
  4. Drew every chart with D3.js and added a View data table.

Limitations

What the data cannot tell you

  • Counts are not adjusted for population, so they are not risk.
  • Each row is a count of admissions, not a patient.
  • Records with a missing age or sex are left out of the age and sex charts.
  • Definitions or recording may have changed over the years.
